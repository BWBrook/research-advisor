## Assignment

Develop this idea independently into the strongest worthwhile, feasible and scientifically defensible investigation it can support. Challenge the initial formulation where useful. Define the intended contribution, not a predetermined result or a detailed implementation plan.

Consider:

1. Which scientific questions are most promising, and why do they matter?
2. What would constitute a substantial contribution rather than a small increment?
3. What minimum defensible outcome would justify calling the project complete? Which optional stretch goals would add real value without preventing completion?
4. What evidence, data and verification would be needed? What would a negative, null or infeasible result mean for a worthwhile conclusion?
5. Which conceptual weaknesses, assumptions, novelty questions, data limitations or resource constraints could require reformulation or make the project unsuitable?

Develop two or three distinct project conceptions and recommend one, or recommend against proceeding if warranted. Be clear about what is established, assumed and still unknown.

You may conduct focused preliminary checks of novelty or feasibility using available sources. Use primary sources for technical claims, cite what you actually inspect, and disclose access limits. Do not let these checks become the investigation. Do not begin coding, run the research programme, create infrastructure or collect a new dataset.

Work independently without consulting other participants or their proposals. Exercise judgement and resolve ordinary uncertainty; ask only about a genuinely essential missing input. Do not infer that success must confirm the original idea.

Return about 1,000-1,500 words. Include a short header identifying the common input, your model/harness if actually known, and `Status: ready for review` or `Status: blocked` with a precise reason. Stop after the brief. The adviser and Barry will use the independent conceptions to establish the next stage.
