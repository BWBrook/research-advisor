## Assignment

This is a mature scientific project whose data, analyses, modelling and existing manuscript are complete. The outstanding problem is communication: the manuscript has accumulated dense, over-qualified prose and needs to be reconceived from the ground up. Treat the scientific work as settled for this assignment. Your task is **independent editorial assessment and conception**, not scientific review, new analysis or full manuscript drafting.

## Reading order

Read only the curated workspace listed in the input packet:

1. **Science and evidence first:** Inspect the figures, claims ledger, interpretation boundaries and primary summary tables *before* reading the old manuscript prose, so your mental model of the paper's contribution forms from the evidence rather than the old structure.
2. **Existing prose second:** Read the existing manuscript text and figure captions as **source material** to ensure no substantive scientific content is lost — never as a structural or stylistic template. Source-backed reading is sufficient; do not rerun or revalidate analyses.

## What to return

Return a self-contained assessment covering:

1. **Contribution:** What question does the work answer, what has it established, and why should the target audience care? Distinguish the central finding from broader implications.
2. **Content to preserve:** Identify the core findings, numerical results, indispensable methods, material qualifications and attribution that a rewrite must retain. Point to source sections, figures, tables or claim IDs. Flag any genuine ambiguity without inventing missing facts.
3. **Diagnosis:** Explain the main obstacles to clarity, narrative arc and persuasive scientific communication in the current manuscript. Focus on structural and rhetorical causes rather than line edits. Also flag any existing phrasing rules or guardrails that over-police prose style beyond what scientific accuracy genuinely requires.
4. **Alternative narratives:** Propose two or three substantially different ways to frame and organise this same science for the target venue. For each, state the opening hook, central question and sequence of evidence. Recommend one and explain the trade-offs.
5. **Prose specimen (150–250 words):** Draft *either* a reworked Abstract *or* the opening two paragraphs of your recommended narrative. This tests actual voice, clarity and precision — not just an outline.
6. **Boundaries:** Identify what a cleaner narrative must not distort or silently omit. Note what technical detail can stay in (or move to) the supplement, tables or figure captions without weakening the main argument. Do not preserve repetition merely because it exists.

## Strictly out of bounds

- **Work independently.** Do not read or consult other scientists, their workspaces, reports, branches or discussion threads.
- **Do not query the Correspondence Board** (via MCP or otherwise), search prior chat transcripts, or rely on prior project memory about this paper. Work strictly from the curated baseline supplied here.
- **Do not inspect files outside your curated workspace** (including the parent scientific repository, `git log`, historical review memos, or out-of-scope renderings such as briefs or supplements unless listed in the input packet).
- **Do not edit source files**, change data or code, run analyses, expand the literature review, or draft the full manuscript. Write only the requested assessment to your output path.

If reading exposes a concrete contradiction that materially affects a central claim, name its exact source and consequence without launching a scientific audit. Label anything not established by the supplied record rather than claiming independent verification.

Aim for about 1,200–1,800 words (including the 150–250 word prose specimen, excluding a compact source table if useful). Start with a short header identifying the source baseline, your actual model and harness if known, and `Status: ready for review` or `Status: blocked` with a precise reason.
