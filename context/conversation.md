### Assistant — edited record

The assistant distinguished research activity from completed contributions. Delegation can increase production faster than the user's capacity to understand, evaluate and integrate the resulting work. Switching projects requires reconstructing their intellectual state, so adding projects may impose more than a simple proportional burden. The assistant explicitly declined to assert a particular negative-exponential law.

The last portion of a project can involve unusually concentrated scientific judgement: deciding what has been established, reconciling results, assessing claims and preparing an output one is willing to stand behind. The assistant used the rhetorical example that the “final 10%” might contain “50% of the intellectual responsibility”; this was an illustration, not an empirical estimate.

Three useful functions were distinguished:

- **Creation:** substantial autonomous work on a defined problem.
- **Independent challenge:** criticism, alternative explanations and reproduction that do not simply inherit the first investigator's assumptions.
- **Scientific integration:** the user's concentrated judgement about evidence, interpretation and direction.

Not every model needs to operate continuously. Different models may be most useful at different moments. The objective is better decisions and finished contributions, not maximised agent-hours.

Completion should include a deliberate public or collaborative disposition. A paper or release merely sitting in a repository may not have reached its intended audience. A well-supported decision not to publish or continue can also finish an investigation; drifting indefinitely is the problem. Archiving ideas preserves curiosity without committing to pursue all of them immediately.

The assistant undertook to treat one to three active projects, completion and personal intellectual ownership as the working default. Its memorable formulation was: “Optimise for completed intellectual contributions, not continuous intellectual activity.”

The assistant proposed shifting the unit of delegation from small tasks to coherent outcomes. The user would invest more effort in defining the scientific objective, standards and boundaries, then let the investigator determine much of the route.

The comparison was:

| Aspect | Frequent incremental handoffs | Larger autonomous assignments |
| --- | --- | --- |

### Assistant — edited record

The assistant supported a trial, with two main modifications:

1. Obtain independent initial conceptions before joint ideation, so the first plausible proposal does not anchor all three models.
2. Use the final council for evidence-based adjudication, not agreement as an end in itself.

The proposed phases were independent conception followed by a shared charter; independent autonomous work; independent written cross-critiques before discussion; adjudication and integration; then fresh communication.

#### Independence and a common destination

Each investigator would receive the same objective and frozen starting material, but retain freedom to choose methods and structure. A short charter could specify the scientific question, minimum worthwhile contribution, ambitious goals, guardrails, verification and completion criteria. It should define the destination without prescribing the route or demanding a desired finding.

The assistant argued that frequent exchanges may create a shared frame in which disagreements concern details rather than fundamental assumptions. Independent investigators might discover different formulations, methods or limits of the question. It also acknowledged that different providers do not guarantee independent errors: models can share training material, assumptions and weaknesses.

The earlier response cited purported 2026 studies in support of caution about multi-agent debate and mentioned unanimously incorrect agreement and cost-effectiveness of independent self-correction. **Those citations were not verified in this handover, and no bibliographic claim is adopted here.** Even as originally stated, the assistant acknowledged that benchmark findings on smaller models would not establish the value of three frontier agents conducting entire research projects. The proposed architecture remains an empirical workflow experiment.

#### Autonomous execution and decision boundaries

For research projects, each model would have the same starting repository and charter, its own worktree or branch, the required resources, isolated outputs and authority to resolve routine problems. A concise record would retain consequential choices, deviations, uncertainty and failed approaches; a transcript of every step was not wanted.

The assistant distinguished ordinary decisions, which the agent should make; consequential scientific choices, which it could make provisionally while documenting reasons and checking sensitivity; and changes to the fundamental research objective, which should be brought back to the user. Inability to achieve the original objective can be a legitimate finding rather than something to conceal.

#### Cross-review and adjudication

Each model would initially assess the other two independently, tying criticisms to evidence, locations or reproducible discrepancies. Only then would there be a bounded exchange to resolve material differences.

Different answers can reflect an error, different assumptions or real sensitivity. They should not be blended into a comfortable compromise without understanding which explanation applies. Unresolved disagreement is permissible. Original results should remain available, and the most attractive story should not win merely because it is attractive. Where relevant, common evaluation standards and protected validation material should be set before seeing results.

Scientific integration does not require merging three complete software architectures. One implementation may become the maintained pipeline, another may supply a specific improvement, and another may remain an independent check. Keeping an independent implementation can be more useful than combining everything into one codebase.

#### Fresh communication

The manuscript should explain the scientific argument, not recapitulate the history of revisions. Accumulated qualifications and fixes can leave a document that contains information but lacks a clear intellectual structure.

A fresh model session and the user could construct the narrative from a compact, evidence-grounded record of the questions, findings, principal figures, limitations, negative results, unresolved issues and reasons for preferring particular interpretations. Clear notes and prototype figures remain useful during research because explanation can expose conceptual problems; deferring manuscript construction does not mean abandoning intelligibility during the investigation.

#### Keeping the process small

The assistant warned that charters, councils, arbitration reports and release gates could recreate the original bureaucracy. It proposed no ongoing inter-agent discussion during independent execution; a bounded review focused on substantive discrepancies; and no new orchestration platform unless experience showed a need. Git worktrees, Markdown and existing harnesses were enough for a first trial.

Suggested evaluation questions were whether independence found a consequential problem or insight, whether claims were reproducible, how much of the user's attention went to science versus supervision, what integration cost arose, and whether the work reached a real endpoint. These were reflection questions, not a formal scoring system.

Three full investigations were not declared universally optimal. One main investigator plus two critics may suit some projects. The roles and number of agents should remain responsive to the problem. The assistant's compressed formulation was: “Deliberate jointly. Investigate independently. Critique independently. Adjudicate on evidence. Communicate afresh.”

The assistant again discussed applying full investigation phases to RC dates. **That part was subsequently corrected by the user in the next turn.**

# Appendix: the original two reconnaissance prompts

These are historical prompt versions, preserved verbatim apart from removing their ChatGPT writing-block wrappers. They are not automatically the latest runnable prompts. ## Original A prompt

```markdown
# Independent scientific narrative assessment

You are examining an advanced scientific research project whose analyses, results and manuscript are substantially complete. The principal outstanding task is to transform the existing research into a compelling, accurate and readable scientific paper.

Your assignment is **independent reconnaissance and editorial conception**, not revision or implementation.

Examine the repository carefully. Where practical, develop your initial understanding from the scientific results, figures and supporting evidence before reading the existing manuscript. Then examine the manuscript to understand the full information content that a replacement must preserve.

Approach the manuscript as source material, not as a structural or stylistic template.

Provide a concise assessment covering:

1. **Scientific contribution:** What has this research established, why does it matter, and what is the strongest defensible central message?
2. **Essential content:** Which findings, methodological details, qualifications and supporting evidence must survive a complete rewrite? Identify their sources in the repository.
3. **Narrative diagnosis:** Why might the existing manuscript be difficult to read or less compelling than the underlying science warrants?
4. **Alternative conceptions:** Propose two or three genuinely different ways of framing and organising the paper. Recommend the strongest, explaining why.
5. **Editorial boundaries:** Identify anything that must not be misrepresented or lost. Flag any genuinely consequential scientific problem, but do not propose additional research merely to refine an already mature project.

**Constraints:** Do not edit files, run new analyses, rewrite the manuscript or communicate with other agents. Treat the established scientific findings as provisionally settled. Distinguish verified findings from claims you could not independently check.

Work autonomously. Make reasoned editorial judgements rather than referring routine decisions back to me.

Return a self-contained assessment of approximately 1,000–1,500 words. We will use it to decide the next stage, not as an instruction to begin drafting.
```

## Original B prompt

```markdown
# Independent scientific project conception

I want to develop an original scientific investigation, beginning with the idea provided below.

Your assignment is to evaluate and develop this idea independently, with the goal of identifying the strongest worthwhile, feasible and scientifically defensible research project it could support.

Think broadly and critically. Do not assume my initial formulation is necessarily the most interesting or productive one.

Consider:

1. What are the most promising scientific questions arising from the idea, and why do they matter?
2. What would constitute a substantial contribution rather than a minor incremental result?
3. What minimum scientific outcome would justify considering the project complete? What ambitious but realistic extensions might substantially increase its importance?
4. What evidence, data, validation and independent checks would be needed to support the eventual conclusions?
5. What major uncertainties, conceptual weaknesses or feasibility constraints could make the project unsuitable or require reformulation?

Develop two or three distinct possible project conceptions, then recommend the strongest—or recommend against pursuing the idea if warranted.

**Constraints:** This is an ideation exercise, not a research implementation. Do not begin coding or create project infrastructure. You may undertake focused preliminary investigation to assess novelty and feasibility, but distinguish established facts from assumptions.

Work independently, without consulting other agents. Exercise scientific judgement, including challenging the premise.

Return a concise research-conception brief, approximately 1,000–1,500 words. Focus on the scientific objective and what a successful completed project would establish, not a prescriptive implementation plan.

## Initial idea

[Insert my scientific idea here.]
```
